# Priority Comparison — Conditional Companion

Read **only** when the Feature Investment Gate must compare at least two real candidate investments for a limited engineering budget. This is not a standalone Skill or a scoring framework. Market collection belongs to `market-intelligence-mining`; feature surface/value to the Feature Investment Gate.

## Compare without a fictitious score

1. **Candidates and constraints**: identify the actual competing proposals, consumer/beneficiary, baseline and scarce resource (engineering time, tokens, attention, dependencies). If there is no known competing candidate, do not pretend there is.
2. **Investment role**: distinguish mandatory **foundation** (reliability, security, performance prerequisite for adoption), **differentiator** (purchase/retention advantage), and **optional embellishment**. Foundation can unlock commercial value without a standalone price.
3. **Comparable evidence**: for each option use only known data: intensity of unmet need, market/commercial signal, strength/freshness of evidence, strategic fit, marginal value, approximate effort/risk, dependencies and reversibility. Contrast equal audiences where possible; do not equate GitHub reactions with paid adoption.
4. **Timing and displacement**: ask what breaks or is lost by waiting; what the investment unlocks; whether it blocks another project; what **specific alternative** gets delayed if this is done now. A valuable idea may lose on timing.
5. **Dominance and uncertainty**: if one candidate dominates on the relevant constraints, explain plainly. For tradeoffs, identify the decisive constraint rather than constructing weights or an arbitrary 0–100 score. When uncertainty cannot be resolved economically, preserve it and favor smaller reversible commitment or DEFER.
6. **Decision**: choose BUILD NOW, DEFER, REJECT or narrower investment as supported; map back to the Gate's formal outcomes and required surface. State why now, what was compared, uncertainty/conflicts, and when to revisit.

## Counterexamples

- Do not prioritize a popular paid feature over a critical indexing failure when the failure prevents the product from functioning.
- Do not rank a feature as commercially weak solely because nobody requests it by name; compare the underlying problem and paid alternatives.
- Do not downgrade every internal feature as unmonetizable: enabling adoption and retention may dominate direct upsell value.
- Do not presume the competitor with the most issues or most stars has the best business opportunity.

## Minimal output

**Priority now:** chosen candidate and investment scope. **Why:** decisive evidence/constraint. **Compared with:** actual alternative(s). **Tradeoff:** what is postponed. **Confidence and revisit trigger:** evidence gap or change that could reverse the choice.

No marketing experiment, landing page or new paid traffic is required. This is a desk-decision over existing evidence.
